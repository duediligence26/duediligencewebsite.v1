Before the tour

A little glossary for curious minds.

Every great trip starts with a few local words. Here are the six you'll hear most around here, so nothing on the tour sounds like a secret code.

Futures

A contract that rides along with a whole market. NQ follows the Nasdaq-100 (home of the big tech names) and ES follows the S&P 500. They're night owls: they trade almost around the clock.

Micro contract

The fun-size version of a futures contract (MNQ, MES): one tenth the size, so every win and every oops is one tenth too. Our results are shown per micro.

Indicator

A helper that draws on a price chart. Ours mark the prices that matter and leave friendly notes like "armed", "stay away" or why today's a rest day. They never trade for you.

Backtest

A time machine for ideas: we replay a rule on years of past prices to see how it would have done. Wonderful for learning, though the future always writes its own story.

Target and stop

The two exits you pick before the ride starts. The target is where you collect the prize; the stop is the safety bar that ends a trade that isn't working.

Options on QQQ and SPY

QQQ and SPY are funds that track those same two markets. Options are contracts on them; a call gains when the price climbs. Some of our alerts point to one worth a look.

Overnight Dip Buy

While most of the world is asleep, markets often wander back down to the previous day's low and then bounce. This tool keeps watch: it marks that low, waits for price to arrive, and plans a small, tidy profit, with a planned exit in case the bounce doesn't show up.

It even takes the night off after a sleepy day, and its "mood light" reads QUIET, NORMAL or ACTIVE before the evening begins.

  1. 01Find yesterday's daytime high and low (9:30am to 4pm, New York time).
  2. 02Play only if tonight starts inside that range, after a day that moved more than usual.
  3. 03Buy at yesterday's low, aim for a small bounce, and be home by 8:25am.

Stop 02 · Studies · Ready now

Sixteen years of homework, with every grade on the fridge.

Every idea takes the same exam: real minute-by-minute prices from 2010 to 2026, fees included, and years it had never seen before. Here's how the class did.

The headliner's report card

The Overnight Dip Buy made money in all six of its tests: three stretches of time on two markets, including years nobody peeked at while designing it.

How to read it: "80 · 88%" means 80 trades, and 88% of them made money. Profit is per micro contract, after a $1.50 fee per trade.

~2× per trade

One small rule, take the night off after a sleepy day, roughly doubled the average profit per trade while trading less than half as often. Rest really does help.

199 rescue tries

Three other ideas lost money before 2021. We tried 199 filters and 104 tweaks to save them. None of them worked, so we print that right on the chart.

+$18,510

Every signal from Oct 2025 to Oct 2026, simulated on 4 micros per market. It wasn't a smooth ride: it dipped as much as $3,108 along the way, and four months ended in the red.

The market's mood light

Wouldn't it be lovely to know ahead of time when an idea tends to shine? We tested eight warning signs, and one kept its promise for the dip idea: how much the market has been moving lately.

On quiet days the idea about broke even; on busy days it did its best work. Think of it as a weather report, not a fortune teller.
